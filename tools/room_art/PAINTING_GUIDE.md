# Painting rooms for After Hours – College Tale

You are painting rooms for a Godot pixel-art RPG (Undertale/Deltarune-like) set on the CU Boulder campus
at night. Three rooms are finished and approved by the game's author, who called them "perfect":
U01 Broadway, U02 UMC terrace, U04 club room. Each also has its own battle backdrop, which he also
approved ("those are all sweet"). Every other room must be painted to the same standard, each with its
own battle backdrop.

Everything is generated by Python scripts in `tools/room_art/` that write PNGs and a JSON manifest
into the Godot project at `assets/art/rooms/<ID>/`.

## 2. Style rules (what was approved)

- **1:1 game pixels.** The game renders at 640x360 and scales up with nearest filtering. Paint at
  exactly the size it is shown. No smooth gradients, no blur, no anti-aliasing, no noise dither.
  Colour comes in clean clusters (3-6 tones per material), with a dark ink outline (#171a2b /
  #1a1524) on props.
- **Mid resolution, genuine detail.** Above the old flat-rectangle look, below the character
  sprites' detail. Bricks, mortar, wood grain, book spines in varied colours, window mullions with
  warm light behind, plants with leaf clusters, posters, clocks, cables, small clutter. No big flat
  areas, but no random speckle either: every detail should read as a thing.
- **Character scale.** World characters are ~52px tall (feet at their y). Doors are about 1.4x a
  character (~72px). Chairs, tables, benches, shelves, lamps all sized against a 52px person. Never
  shrink a whole building to fit the screen (that made doors half a character tall and was rejected);
  show part of a facade at true scale instead.
- **Night at dusk.** The world is tinted by CanvasModulate (0.68, 0.72, 0.84), so paint brighter and
  warmer than it will appear; the preview applies the tint. Warm amber lamp light (#f6cf7a, #e9a84a)
  against cool plum/indigo shadow. Light pools on floors under lamps (`surfaces.light_pool`).
- **Text** uses the game font via `pixel.text(cv, s, x, y, colour)` (6px advance, about 8px tall).
  Keep signs short, upper case, legible, never mirrored.
- **Palette** families already exist in `props.py`, `interior.py`, `facade.py` and `surfaces.py`;
  reuse them so rooms feel like one world, and add new ones in your own lib file.

## 3. The room canvas and its layout

From rooms.json, for room R:

- `dimensions` [W, H]: the background PNG is exactly W x H (640x360, 800x480 or 960x540). The camera
  scrolls over rooms larger than 640x360, so the whole canvas is seen.
- `walk_bounds` [x, y, w, h]: where feet can go. Everything above `y` is the back wall / facade / sky
  (a character standing at the top edge has their head about 52px above it). The floor covers the
  walk bounds; darken the margins outside it (see build_u04.py).
- `placements[]`: furniture and fixtures, each {kind, x, y, w, h, depth, object, label}. (x, y) is
  the floor point: bottom-centre of a w x h box. A placement with depth > 0 is solid: its collision
  box is (x - w/2, y - depth, w, depth). **Only placements are solid**, so anything you paint into
  the background on the walkable floor must be flat (rugs, paving patterns, tape marks, light pools,
  leaves, cables). Tall things on the floor must be placement props.
- `objects[]`: interactions. NPCs (`sprite` key, or kind talk/battle with a sprite) stand at (x, y);
  keep a clear ~50x60 area there and do not paint people. Doors with `to` lead to other rooms.
- Keepsakes: small pickups (about 32x30, glinting on the floor) appear at fixed points that are not in
  rooms.json. Keep these spots free of props and occluders: U01 (52, 204), U04 (60, 320),
  U05 (580, 264), U07 (60, 322), F03 (748, 302), F05 (748, 294), N03 (60, 334), N05 (60, 318),
  E02 (748, 326).
- Lamps: each `lamp` placement also makes a real point light at (x, y - h + 5), so the lamp head of
  your sprite should sit at about h - 5 above its floor point.
- N04 shelves have `base_x` and `shift_x`: when flag `stacks_shifted` is set the shelf moves to
  base_x + shift_x. They must be props, and the background behind both positions must look right.
  Use `python3 preview.py room N04 out.png stacks_shifted=true` to see the shifted state.
- rooms.json is read-only for you. Do not move placements or objects.

### What each placement becomes

For every placement index, choose exactly one:

1. **Prop sprite** (`props` in the manifest): a sprite drawn with its anchor pixel on (x, y), y-sorted
   with the characters by y. Painters return `(Canvas, anchor_x, anchor_y)` (see props.py). Size it
   to roughly w wide and h tall.
2. **Painted into the background** and listed in `label_only`: for things on the back wall (doors,
   stair openings, arches, wall boards). The game then draws only a small label pill centred at
   (x, y - h - 20) with the placement's label, red while the exit is locked, so paint the doorway's
   top near y - h. **Doors and stairs always go this way.**
3. **Left to code** (no art, not in label_only): `threshold` (exit mats drawn by code at the room
   edge; paint the path or opening leading there), `booth`, `arcade`, `scoreboard`, `return`,
   `bridge_model`, `pip_table`. These draw live content; keep their footprint clear in the background.

Kinds that draw state details on top of their art, relative to the anchor in the placement's w x h
frame (so the sprite must fill exactly that box, anchored bottom-centre): `flyer_note`, `bike`,
`stage` with object "stage", `table` with object "invitation", `ticket`, `shelf` with object
"lost_labels", `cart` with object "mags_checklist". Read `_draw_state_details` in
`scenes/spatial_prop.gd` before painting one of these.

Signs and notices: the procedural version printed the placement's `label`. When you paint a sign,
notice or panel as a prop, paint its label text into it if it fits (it is what the player reads).

A prop can swap sprite with a flag: `{"texture", "anchor", "flag": "mixer_returned", "flag_texture"}`
shows flag_texture while the flag is truthy.

## 4. Manifest format (returned by build(project) in build_<id>.py)

```json
{
 "background": "res://assets/art/rooms/R/background.png",
 "background_dawn": "res://assets/art/rooms/R/background-dawn.png",
 "width": 960,
 "props": {"3": {"texture": "res://.../prop-3.png", "anchor": [40, 61]}},
 "label_only": [0, 2],
 "occluders": [{"texture": "res://.../front-pillar.png", "x": 100, "y": 380, "base": 470}],
 "overlays": [{"texture": "res://.../sign-open.png", "x": 300, "y": 110, "flag": "stacks_shifted"},
              {"texture": "res://.../sign-crank.png", "x": 300, "y": 110, "flag": "stacks_shifted", "when": false},
              {"texture": "res://.../x.png", "x": 0, "y": 0, "flag": "flyer_resolution", "equals": "peaceful"}],
 "fauna": [{"kind": "squirrel", "x": 180, "y": 273, "range": 3, "speed": 0.25, "rate": 1.2},
           {"kind": "bird", "x": 60, "y": 38, "fly": 9.0, "rate": 4.0}],
 "leaves": [[40, 30, 90, 70]],
 "layers": [{"kind": "blink", "pattern": "10", "rate": 2, "rects": [[300, 60, 3, 2, "8ca992"]], "flag": "source_reel"}]
}
```

- `background_dawn` (optional): used instead of `background` once flag `dawn_started` is set.
- `occluders`: painted pieces drawn in front of characters standing behind them; y-sorted on `base`,
  drawn with top-left at (x, y). For columns, foreground tree trunks, a balcony rail at the front.
- `overlays`: images drawn over the background when their condition holds. Condition: `flag` plus
  either `equals` (string compare) or `when` (true = flag set, false = flag unset; default true).
- `fauna`: animals from the shared sheet (`squirrel`, `rabbit`, `pigeon`, `bird`). Walkers sway
  `range` px at `speed`; `fly` ones cross the room width and wrap; `rate` is frames per second;
  (x, y) is the feet. To add animals (moths, bats, ducks, a cat, an owl, a deer), create
  `fauna_extra_<group>.py` with `KINDS = [(name, frames, palette)]`, frames being lists of equal-size
  strings and palette a dict char -> hex ("." is transparent). See fauna.py for the format.
  All these files feed one shared sheet and a name defined twice is overwritten, so reuse kinds
  other groups already made (see `fauna_sheet()` regions) and give new kinds unique names.
- `leaves`: [x, y, w, h] areas where a few leaves fall (outdoor rooms with trees).
- `layers`: animated layers, same kinds as battle backdrops (section 6), in room pixels. Any layer may
  carry the same `flag` / `equals` / `when` condition as overlays.
- The `battle` section is added by build_all.py from build_battle_<id>.py; do not write it yourself.

## 6. Battle backdrops

A battle starts in the room where the player met the enemy. Its backdrop is a different, more
interesting view from inside that room, in the style of Final Fantasy VI battle backgrounds: a
one-point perspective scene the fighters stand in, with things moving. The author's words: "dont just
have the same background make a new one for each room that is more interesting".

Screen layout (640x360) during battle:

- Party fighters stand on the left at x = 55 + i*65 (i = 0..3), feet at y 150, about 72px tall.
- The enemy stands at (480, 146), 80-104px tall.
- UI covers: pause button x 12-65, y 8-25; encounter box x 392-628, y 8-70; command panels from y 166
  down to the bottom; the dodge arena box is (192, 166) 256x120.
- So the band y 0-166 is the picture; keep the ground line around y 150 readable, keep strong detail
  out from behind the fighters' heads where possible, and continue the floor down to 360 darkening
  (`v.rows(156, 360, INK, 0, 0.62)`), because gaps between panels show it.

Tools (`persp.py`): `View(horizon=98, cam=52)`; the fighters' line is depth Z = F = 320 at y 150.
`v.ground(shader(X, Z))` paints the floor, `v.plane(A, B, shader(u, Y, Z), y_max)` paints any vertical
wall between ground points A=(X, Z) and B, `v.back(Z, shader(X, Y))` a back wall, `v.sprite(rgba,
X, Z, ...)` places a painted sprite at depth (pieces painted at room scale need
`scale=ROOM_TO_BATTLE * ...`, see the U02/U01 scripts), `v.strip()` a sky strip, `v.reduce(112)`
quantises to the final Canvas. `fog`, `warm_light`, `hash2`, `texture_lookup` help shaders. Crisp
details (string lights, neon letters) go on the reduced Canvas at 1:1 afterwards.

Outputs: `battle-far.png` (full frame), optional `battle-near.png` (same frame with sky pixels
transparent, from `v.solid_mask()`, needed only when far layers such as clouds or birds must pass
behind scenery), extra textures for layers. build(project) returns:

```json
{"far": "res://.../battle-far.png", "near": "res://.../battle-near.png",
 "far_dawn": "...", "near_dawn": "...",
 "layers": [ ... ]}
```

`far_dawn` / `near_dawn` replace far/near when `dawn_started` (M01 and any Macky room whose view
changes at dawn; M07 is painted at dawn as its normal far image).

Layer kinds (each may have `"depth": "far"` to draw between far and near; default near):

- `drift` {texture, y, speed, alpha}: a horizontally tileable strip scrolling and wrapping (clouds).
- `fauna` {fauna: [...]}: animals, same entries as rooms.
- `twinkle` {points: [[x, y, "hex", size]], rate, min}: breathing lamps, bulbs, stars.
- `blink` {pattern "1010", rate, texture + x, y and/or rects [[x, y, w, h, "hex", alpha]]}: shown
  while pattern[int(t * rate) % len] == "1". Several blink layers with offset patterns make chases,
  waveforms, signal lights.
- `beam` {x, y, angle, sweep, period, phase, length, width, color, alpha, floor}: swinging spotlight.
- `particles` {style "leaf" | "wind" | "dust", count, rect [x, y, w, h], speed [vx, vy], color}.
- `movers` {from [x, y], to [x, y], count, period, size [s0, s1], pair, color, ease "in" | "out"}:
  lights travelling along a line, growing or shrinking (car lights).

Reduced-motion players see one still at t = 1.3. Keep motion gentle and pixel-crisp.

Make each battle different: a new angle on the room (down the reading-room aisles toward the great
window; on the stage looking out at the lawn; between the stacks; over the orchestra pit into the
house). Use what the room is about.

## 7. Process

For each room you own:

1. Read the room's layout, objects, dialogue, the old capture `rooms_now/<ID>.png`, and the state
   visuals table.
2. Write `build_<id>.py` (lower-case id) with `build(project)` that writes the background, props and
   any overlay/layer textures into `<project>/assets/art/rooms/<ID>/` and returns the manifest dict.
   Follow build_u04.py's structure. Use deterministic seeds.
3. Write `build_battle_<id>.py` with `build(project)` that writes the battle images and returns the
   battle dict. Follow build_battle_u02.py / u04 / u01.
4. Run `python3 build_all.py <ID>`, which writes manifest.json.
5. Check with your own eyes, at 2x:
   `python3 preview.py room <ID> tools/room_art/check/<ID>-room.png [flags]` and
   `python3 preview.py battle <ID> tools/room_art/check/<ID>-battle.png [dawn] [dodge]`,
   then Read the PNGs. For rooms larger than 640x360 also look at crops at 1x. Iterate until it
   matches the reference rooms. Check: character scale against the cyan markers, no grey boxes left
   except code-drawn kinds, doors where the label pills will be, nothing solid-looking painted on the
   walkable floor, text legible and not mirrored, every state in section 5 previewed.

Rules:

- New shared helpers go in `lib_<group>.py` (group: umc, farrand, norlin, eng, oldmain, macky). Your
  room and battle scripts import from it and from the existing modules.
- **Do not edit** existing modules: pixel.py, midlib.py, surfaces.py, sky.py, interior.py, props.py,
  facade.py, fauna.py, clouds.py, persp.py, preview.py, build_all.py, or any other room's scripts or
  folders. Other painters are working in parallel. If an existing helper is almost right, copy it into
  your lib and change the copy.
- Do not run Godot, the game harness, or git, and do not touch anything outside
  `tools/room_art/` and your rooms' folders in `assets/art/rooms/`.
- Keep each script readable: short comments that say what is painted, named constants for layout.
